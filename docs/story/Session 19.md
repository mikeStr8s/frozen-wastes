# Session 19: Thunder, Ale, and the Dragon's Answer

The party returned to [Bremen](../world/atlas/Bremen.md) with [Spellix Romwood](../world/characters/Spellix%20Romwood.md) in tow, the Shield Guardian stomping along behind them — its iron frame still covered head to toe in goblin graffiti. Spellix thanked them with genuine warmth, explained he would be staying at the inn briefly before heading south to his family, then turned out his pockets to reveal they were entirely empty. He shrugged. Perhaps the inn was too much to hope for.

[Aronious](../world/characters/Aronious.md) pressed two gold into his hand without a word. Rest, at least, would come easy.

[Dzaan](../world/characters/Dzaan.md) was sent ahead to the inn as well. He walked through the door, sat down at the nearest table, and immediately began scrawling in his spellbook with the focused desperation of a man racing against his own memory. He did not look up.

[Yin Shen](../world/characters/Yin%20Shen.md) remarked that it was past time they all rested — everyone had taken a beating. [Hobbins](../world/characters/Hobbins.md) and Aronious turned to look at her with the flat, unified expression of two people covered in bruises regarding someone without a single mark on them. Yin Shen said nothing further.

---

Before rest could be considered, Hobbins had unfinished business.

The Shield Guardian stood in the street where they had left it, patient and enormous and absolutely covered in goblin scrawl. She was not willing to leave it that way. The party browsed the shelves of the local market, where they found a bottle of *Grizwald's Famous Degreaser* with the tagline: *It'll remove anything and everything.* The clerk admitted, without any apparent shame, that it was in fact terrible at removing grease but cut through paint as though it had never existed.

Too harsh. They put it back.

Instead they selected green, pink, purple, and black paint and a set of brushes, and set about the rather more delicate work of making the construct look less like a siege weapon and more like something a person might not immediately flee from. Hobbins applied the finishing touch herself: a pink peace symbol, carefully painted across the Guardian's forehead.

It helped. Somewhat.

---

Yin Shen suggested, again, that they head inside to rest. Aronious made only a token effort to stop Hobbins from lunging at Yin Shen's ankles, his grip conspicuously loose as the ferret squeaked about not a single arrow having touched her, about bruises, about the Shield Guardian nearly being destroyed. The scuffle resolved itself in time, and the party made for their rooms.

They had barely started up the stairs when the door burst open.

[Mylbor Tarrfak](../world/characters/Mylbor%20Tarrfak.md) filled the frame, crimson-stained sack slung over one shoulder and Thunder Stick raised in one hand — or what remained of it. The end had mushroomed outward into a tangle of singed and curling metal.

He grinned the grin of a man who had fired his only shot and found it sufficient.

"Only took one shot," he said, dropping the sack on the floor with a wet thud. "Though I only had one shot." A hearty laugh filled the room.

He threw his coin purse onto the bar before anyone could object, ordered a round, and passed tankards to Yin Shen and Aronious. Aronious, with careful deliberation, poured a bottle cap's worth of clear liquor for Hobbins.

They raised their drinks. Mylbor drained his in a single pull and looked around the table with the expectant expression of a man waiting to be matched.

Aronious, reasoning that a sufficiently drunk Mylbor might prove a more cooperative ally, accepted the implied challenge. He seized a full bottle of clear liquor and poured it down without pause. Mylbor turned to the barmaid and asked what that equated to in ale. She said a full cask. Mylbor's eyes lit up.

He was already behind the bar and on the floor before anyone thought to stop him. Yin Shen grabbed the handle of the keg and opened it. Mylbor sputtered, drank, gulped, and sputtered again — and eventually, with great reluctance, tapped the floor.

He stood slowly, thumped his chest, and produced a belch of considerable authority.

"You win, laddy."

Yin Shen suggested it was time for everyone to sleep. Hobbins, no longer able to contain herself on the subject of Yin Shen's perfect, unmarked, unscathed person, launched herself across the room. The scuffle was brief. Things settled. The party went to bed.

---

They rose early, waited for Mylbor to surface, and found him in reasonable shape for a man who had consumed a cask of ale. Over breakfast, he and Yin Shen came to an agreement: they would stay in contact. She wanted word of the ancient white dragon that roamed Icewind Dale. He wanted word of anything worth hunting. Both parties shook on it and parted ways.

---

The road to [Termalaine](../world/atlas/Termalaine.md) ate most of the day, and the party arrived in the evening to find a large orc in fine clothing standing in the street barking orders at a cluster of civilians doing their best impression of a town militia. Most of them looked as though they had never held a weapon before last week.

He turned when the party approached. They explained they were working under [Captain Imdra Arlaggath](../world/characters/Captain%20Imdra%20Arlaggath.md) and were looking for the town Speaker.

"You've found him," he said, and introduced himself as [Oarus Masthew](../world/characters/Oarus%20Masthew.md). Imdra's warning had reached him, and he had wasted no time. Every able-bodied person in Termalaine who could hold a spear was now, in some technical sense, a soldier. The party left him to it.

---

Finding a quiet corner to think, the group turned to a question that had been waiting long enough: the ancient white dragon.

Yin Shen was confident. White dragons, she explained, were benevolent creatures. They enjoyed the company of people.

Hobbins composed herself, reached for the magic of *sending*, and pushed the message outward into the cold.

*"Don't panic, we are friendly, need help, Ten-Towns in danger, meet at Redwaters, Lupine can vouch, at sundown in two days. Ciao!"*

The answer came back low and rasping, like stone grinding against stone.

*"Come to my lair at the Sea of Moving Ice. You'll find a shipwreck I use as one of my homes."*

Two days of travel, at minimum. The direction was vague. The invitation was not the kind one declined.

---

As they moved through Termalaine's streets turning the logistics over in their minds, a posting on the [Gelp](../world/organizations/Gelp.md) board stopped them short. Written in urgent, uneven letters: *Miners Lost! 50g Reward.* Below it, in smaller text, a note that the local miners' guild had refused all work until the lost men were found and the mine declared safe.

Fifty gold was fifty gold. They looked at one another, looked at the road stretching north toward the Sea of Moving Ice, and thought about it for a longer moment than any of them would readily admit.

The dragon was waiting.

They turned up their collars and walked back out into the cold.

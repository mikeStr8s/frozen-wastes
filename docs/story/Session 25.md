# Session 25: Crew Safe, Vessel in Peril

Before the inn, and before anyone slept, there was the rubble.

Bucket chains worked through the ruined district of [Easthaven](../world/atlas/Easthaven.md), passing snow hand to hand onto the fires. The party and [Polard](../world/characters/Polard%20Hinscrop.md) went through what the dragon had left, pulling out the pinned and the unconscious. Most of them, once dug free, turned out to be unhurt.

Then Polard reached his own lane. The radiant breath had gone straight down it, and not one building stood.

He went to his knees and wept.

[Hobbins](../world/characters/Hobbins.md) asked whether the shop had been insured. Polard took out the pamphlet the party had given him, turned to the last page, and showed them the checklist. The final box was empty.

"I never got it notarized."

---

A week later the plot was twice the size it had been, Easthaven's thanks for his part in the defense. For the moment it held a tent, a sign, a freshly built workbench, and no merchandise of any kind.

Polard told them he was stepping back from adventuring. A hiatus. A temporary retirement, as he put it. He meant to install a forge and train with the Easthaven blacksmith, so that whatever gear the party needed, he could supply.

[Aronious](../world/characters/Aronious.md) asked after his brother.

Polard had no brother. He said he would invite him up from the south. [Yin Shen](../world/characters/Yin%20Shen.md) bore this with open exasperation.

He cried as they left, and asked them to visit often.

---

Yin Shen said she wanted to do some shopping.

She took four items to a middling jeweler's shop and asked for a private appraisal. She watched him as he looked them over. He was not obviously lowballing her. He was very interested. When he named a large sum, she did not counter.

He paid with a stamped banker's note. She walked it to Easthaven's small bank and deposited the money.

---

It had been a long day of helping Polard rebuild. The inn was giving the town's defenders complimentary crackers and cheese, and Aronious settled in front of the fire with his share and dozed off.

Hobbins saw her chance and slipped out, bound for a bar across town.

---

The bar was an old dog sled kennel: an empty shack fifteen feet across, with a hatch in the floor. Beneath the hatch, a metal spiral staircase wound down into a cavern that had no business fitting under it, packed with partygoers from more regions than Easthaven usually sees in a year.

The host was a gnome wizard in the same robe and the same hat as Hobbins. He worked the room with finger guns, and out of them came confetti, balloons, and mage hands. He conjured her a clear shot. She chugged it, failed to keep it down, and vomited in the bathroom.

It cleared her head. She knew him. [Terence Brightbrow](../world/characters/Terence%20Brightbrow.md), party boy of the university in Waterdeep, who had bullied her for being a nerd.

He denied it.

"Hey, don't try and harsh my mellow. I'm here for a party for everybody."

Then his finger slipped, and a streamer unfurled across the room. It read _no nerds allowed_.

Terence bought the room a round, and every eye in the cavern began to glow blue. Hobbins left on friendly terms, with a bottle of the same white liquor under her arm. If he wanted to find her, she told him, he could ask the townsfolk.

---

She was still on the stairs when six Easthaven guards burst into the shack above. Unpaid taxes. Cudgels only.

Hobbins got into a box, pulled hay over herself, and watched through the slats. The out-of-town guests left by portal. The guards came back up with nothing but Easthaven locals, and with no explanation for why the place had been bigger a moment ago.

When they were gone, she went back down. The staircase ended in the kennel's cold-storage cellar, frozen and small.

Something like _mighty fortress_, she decided. Eighth-level conjuration, from a man whose reputation did not reach anywhere near it.

She took some of the frozen dog food for the sled dogs.

---

Aronious woke to an empty room.

Nobody had seen Hobbins. Someone could point him toward where Yin Shen had been, though, and from there her footprints were as good as a map. He reached the bank square in time to watch her walk out of the bank, and she did not see him.

He followed the trail backward to the jeweler: a short, stout dwarf in a monocle and a ragged top hat. It took nine gold, all told, before the dwarf brought out what she had sold him. A platinum pipe with a carved sapphire bowl. A malachite comb set with red garnets. A pile of small gemstones. A pair of black crystalline slippers. The lot was going south with the next caravan.

Aronious's face did the rest of the negotiating. The jeweler, cowed, pressed a business card into his hand. On the back was a coupon: twenty-five percent off the first gold piece of any purchase over five thousand.

---

He found her on a bench near the bank, counting gold.

He sat down beside her, friendly as anything. Then he gripped her shoulder and cast _alarm_. She had one minute.

She admitted the pieces had come from the chest in the poison room. A couple hundred gold, she said.

"I almost died, okay? You trapped me in that room with the poison. I deserve this."

Then she stabbed a tranquilizer dart into his neck.

He was out for a few seconds. When he came to, exhausted, she was running. _Thorn whip_ missed her once, then again. So he called his companion back as a large eagle, and it buffeted her down the street the way she had come.

Hobbins arrived and dropped a _hypnotic pattern_ into the alley. It caught them both. Aronious managed one half-hearted spank.

Yin Shen, charmed, could not lie. The sale had been worth a lot. The money was in the bank. Hobbins leaned on her until she agreed to share, and Aronious and Hobbins each came away with two hundred gold.

She never did say what the total was.

---

They took the dog sleds north, toward the pull Yin Shen felt, with the Shield Guardian in tow. Aronious had stored a divine smite in it and mentioned this to no one. A long rest on the trail cleared his exhaustion.

The ping in Yin Shen's skull had been there since she took the crystal. As she concentrated, the static thinned, and a gooey voice came through it. A nautiloid was down. Emergency protocols were enabled. The crew was safe, but the vessel was in peril. A psi crystal was needed at once.

She told the others where they were going. They knew what a nautiloid was. Aronious and Hobbins objected, strongly.

Yin Shen kept going.

---

From a rise in the far north they looked down into a small snowy valley. The ship jutted from the snow, half grown and half built, shaped like a cephalopod and glowing. Its tentacles were as thick as tree trunks and coated in mucus that stayed liquid despite the cold. It looked as though someone had tried to bury it.

The broadcast repeated. The same words, every time.

Hobbins wanted no part of it and said they would stay on the sidelines. Yin Shen headed in.

The snow opened beside her. Two large larval things with tentacles for faces came up out of it, and she went rigid where she stood. They dragged her under and toward the ship.

Aronious's _thorn whip_ caught her by the ankle and wrenched her free. The creatures did not turn back for her. They kept burrowing toward the nautiloid.

Hobbins summoned an aberrant spirit to stand guard. It took the shape of a mind flayer. She named it Steve.

Yin Shen lay face down in the snow, unable to comment.

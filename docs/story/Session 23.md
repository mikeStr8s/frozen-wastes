# Session 23: The Heart of Sunblight

The western hallway led them to a door, and the door led them to something none of them had expected.

The woman inside was old in the way that certain things are old — not diminished by it, but concentrated. Long black hair shot through with ribbons of white fell past her shoulders, and her fingernails curved like shards of dark iron. Before her spread a hearty buffet of cooked meats, mushrooms, and stranger fare from deeper places that none of the party cared to identify. She looked up when the door opened, assessed them in a single sweep, and gestured to the empty chairs at her table.

Her guards received a different signal. Weapons away. Back to cooking.

[Hobbins](../world/characters/Hobbins.md) sat down and, in the spirit of diplomacy, forced a single bite of something past her lips. She excused herself from the room with as much dignity as the situation allowed and returned somewhat paler.

The woman introduced herself as [Grandolpha Muzgardt](../world/characters/Grandolpha%20Muzgardt.md) — paramour of [Xardorok Sunblight](../world/characters/Xardorok%20Sunblight.md), the Duergar lord whose fortress they were currently standing inside. She said it without embarrassment, and what followed explained why. She had no allegiance to him worth the name. For some time she had been working from within his command, quietly pulling Duergar loyalties toward herself and away from his ambitions. Her intention, once the ground was ready, was to withdraw entirely — to take her people back into the Underdark and breathe life into the failing realm Xardorok had abandoned when the surface offered him grander dreams of conquest.

She was not an ally by any warm definition of the word. But her interests and theirs pointed, for the moment, in the same direction.

The party asked if her guards could arrange a shift change — enough cover to get them into the lower levels and find [Imdra](../world/characters/Captain%20Imdra%20Arlaggath.md) before Xardorok knew anyone was looking. Grandolpha agreed, and they made a plan. Her men would escort the party in as though they belonged there. From the prison cells, the rest would follow.

---

The shift change moved them through a service elevator and into the lower stronghold, past a cluster of throne room guards bearing Xardorok's sigil who refused the unscheduled rotation without a word and without moving. Grandolpha's escort navigated around them with the practiced ease of people accustomed to working inside a command structure while quietly subverting it.

They were brought through the main forge.

The ceiling ranged forty feet at its peak and pressed down to twenty along the southern wall, the whole cavern swimming in heat and smoke. At its center stood a pyramid forge ten feet tall with its apex sheared off, radiating ripples of firelight from above and emitting a sound from deep within its stone — a slow, deep pulse, like something breathing. A trough ran east from the forge's base, black anvils ranged along either side.

And chained beneath an opening in the ceiling, runic links burning against dark crystal hide, was the dragon.

It was vast and black and furious in the particular way of something powerful that has been made to hold still. Duergar ringed it in a chanting circle, voices locked to the rhythm of the runes. On a raised platform above it all, [Xardorok Sunblight](../world/characters/Xardorok%20Sunblight.md) watched his work with the satisfaction of a man who has built something terrible and knows it.

The escort moved the party through quickly, and the forge fell behind them.

---

Imdra was in the cells. They found her, opened the lock, and put a weapon in her hand before they said much else. She looked at them with the expression of someone filing away questions for later and nodded that she was ready.

---

The plan took shape in whispers. Grandolpha's Duergar would reposition alongside the chanting circle. Hobbins would conceal herself in the beard of one of the allied guards and ride him into range of the chanters — close enough for her most powerful magic to end as many of them as possible in a single moment. [Aronious](../world/characters/Aronious.md) and [Yin Shen](../world/characters/Yin%20Shen.md) would hold back and handle what remained at range. Once the chanters were clear, all fire would turn to Xardorok while the allied Duergar secured the chains. Hobbins would not move until the signal — until [Polard](../world/characters/Polard%20Hinscrop.md), [Mylbor](../world/characters/Mylbor%20Tarrfak.md), and the Shield Guardian had fought their way through the throne room and were in the forge.

They waited. Then the doors at the far end of the forge burst open.

---

Hobbins moved the moment they did. Her magic erupted through the chanting circle, two of the four Duergar dropping before the sound of it finished echoing through the cavern. Aronious took a third. The fourth still held the chain as the allied Duergar surged forward to take up the rhythm of the chant, fingers finding the runic links, voices stumbling into the pattern the dead men had left unfinished. The runes flickered. The dragon thrashed against its bonds with renewed fury.

Yin Shen opened the grey bag of tricks and emptied it at Xardorok — animals surging up the platform in a snarling tide, buying seconds and forcing his attention. Then she raised Dzaan's fireball scroll and read it aloud.

The explosion was effective. Xardorok turned his black crystal gauntlet toward her and hellfire tore open the floor beneath her feet from somewhere else entirely, from a plane that had no business being involved in this fight.

Polard's armor blazed to life. Mylbor raised what remained of his Thunder Stick and waded in. The Shield Guardian hammered into the melee alongside Aronious's spirit reindeer, the forge filling with the noise of a battle that had been building since [Easthaven](../world/atlas/Easthaven.md) burned.

Hobbins sent a spell at Xardorok.

He turned the gauntlet on her.

The hellfire came up beneath her small body and the party watched her collapse, singed and still, to the stone floor.

The fight continued around her.

Aronious broke from his position and ran. He reached her and pressed his hand to her chest and called on something older than the battle around him — Sylvanas, god of nature, the same force that had touched the reindeer and the Chwinga and the herd in the snow. Healing energy passed through his palm and into her.

Hobbins drew a shuddering breath and came back.

---

Xardorok went invisible. It bought him nothing. Yin Shen had him bound with _witch bolt_, the arc of lightning marking him in the dark, and Aronious laid a _hunter's mark_ on the same thread. He emerged from the shroud on the far side of the forge and threw a lever set into the wall. Ice cascaded through the hole in the ceiling in a grinding rush.

He was still pulling it when the party descended on him — spell and arrow and blade converging without pause or mercy. He fell beside his lever, alone in the way that men who trade loyalty for power tend to find themselves at the end.

---

The chains went slack.

The dragon's runic bonds flickered once and failed. It raised its head, drew breath, and exhaled — not ice, but radiant light, a burst that vaporized the nearest constructs in a single sweep. Its wings snapped to their full width, filling the forge with the crack of displaced air, and with one powerful surge it launched itself upward through the hole in the ceiling and out into the open sky above Icewind Dale.

The forge settled into silence around the party. Smoke drifted. Somewhere in the stone above them, the sound of wingbeats faded north.

Grandolpha's Duergar held the chains that no longer bound anything.

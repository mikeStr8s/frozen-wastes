# Tinjong

_Verbeeg, Servant of Sylvanas_

A nine-foot-tall giant in thick walrus hide armor bounds across the ice with surprising speed and grace despite her gangly, misshapen limbs. Although her narrow face is hard to see under the fur-lined hood of her frost-covered cloak, it's clear that the left side of her face sags much lower than the right.

She fills her cave the way certain people fill a room — not through effort, but through simple fact of presence.

## The Rescue

The party met her in the fog outside a shipwreck on the **Sea of Moving Ice**, with an ancient white dragon's head pushing in through the breach behind them.

> "I think you could use some help."

A fog cloud rolled through the hold in a single dense surge. _"To me!"_ She was already shouldering her club and shield into carry position when they reached her, and did not wait for introductions.

> "Quick. My cave."

They ran. The dragon answered its own anger with a blast of breath aimed at the sky, and spears of ice came down like thrown lances the whole way. Tinjong set her club and shield against the cave wall afterward with the unhurried manner of someone returning from an unremarkable errand.

As she straightened, a [Chwinga](../creatures/Chwinga.md) emerged from behind a low rock shelf, its face marked with a pattern the party found immediately and inexplicably familiar. She extended her little finger and the creature touched it gently — a greeting between two beings who had clearly known each other a long time.

## Where She Stands

Tinjong is a servant of **Sylvanas**, god of nature. She was plain about the limits of that: the fight between Ten-Towns and the Duergar is not her concern, and she made no apology for it.

The Rime is another matter. An endless, unnatural winter pressed against the grain of the world itself is exactly her concern, and anything that moves against [Auril](../deities/Auril.md) can count on her when the time comes.

[Aronious](Aronious.md) spent some time in quiet conversation with her before the party left — the particular kind of exchange that passes between two people who understand the wild and are not in a hurry.

Session 7: The Gnome's Gambit and the Spider's Den

The morning after their harrowing encounter with Lupine found the party seeking respite at The White Lady Inn. The innkeeper, recognizing them from their previous stay, offered a generous discount — one silver rather than the usual five — a small kindness in a land that offered few.

While Yin Shen retreated to their room to nurse her wounds and gather her strength, Hobbins enlisted Aronious for a task both peculiar and demanding. Without explanation, she led him back across the frozen expanse of Lac Dinneshere.

There, beneath the gray and endless sky, Hobbins commanded him to cut through the ice.

For an hour, Aronious swung his blade, each strike sending chips and frost flying, until at last he broke through four feet of frozen lake. Before he could catch his breath, Hobbins launched herself toward the opening, the Sea Hag's Frozen Eye clutched in her outstretched paw. Aronious caught her mid-leap, stopping her just an inch above the black water below.

"Don't move a muscle," Hobbins squeaked through chattering teeth, "for one hour."

And so Aronious stood, bent double over the hole, muscles screaming in protest, while Hobbins plunged her tiny paws into the frigid depths. The eye turned and shifted beneath the surface, as if searching, seeking, seeing. The ferret's digits turned blue with cold, her body trembling with exhaustion, but she did not yield.

At last, the eye went still. The cloudiness returned to its cornea, and understanding flooded through her. She had attuned.

Aronious straightened slowly, every joint and sinew crying out in relief. Together, they returned to the inn.

Rested and resolved, the party agreed their next task was clear: they would hunt Skree, the deranged bat godling whose madness threatened the fragile balance of the awakened.

Yin Shen said nothing, but her mind was already turning. Without a word, she rose and strode into the streets of Easthaven. Hobbins and Aronious exchanged a glance, shrugged, and followed.

The fox-spirited rogue led them down a narrow alley to a modest storefront: Strings and Sundries, a shop of wood and string instruments. Inside, a gnome with a bright red shirt greeted them cheerfully. His name, he said, was Finkle Shortfuse.

Yin Shen asked for cymbals.

Finkle apologized profusely but assured her that his cousin down the road would have exactly what she needed. He handed her a coupon — a scrap of old newspaper with the words "ONE DISCOWNT" scrawled in crooked letters.

The party walked to the neighboring shop: Drums Emporium. As they entered, a commotion sounded from the back room, and out stepped an identical gnome — save for the blue shirt he wore. His name, he claimed, was Hansel Wiresnap.

Hobbins' whiskers twitched.

What followed was a comedy of suspicion and investigation. The party moved between the two shops, watching the "cousins" vanish into back rooms and reappear moments later in different colored shirts. During the commotion of moving between stores and multiple conversations, Hobbins and Yin Shen swiped cymbals and a violin from the store shelves. At last, Aronious summoned his dire wolf companion and, with no small amount of indignation from the gnome, carried Finkle-Hansel bodily from one store to the other, proving once and for all that they were the same person.

Cornered, the gnome confessed.

He had opened Strings and Sundries first, but business had been poor. Believing competition would drive customers, he opened a second shop — only to realize that selling different merchandise meant no competition at all. Now he had two failing businesses and no time to run either properly. The charade of being "cousins" was a desperate attempt to make the shops feel like a thriving family enterprise.

Hobbins, ever practical, offered him advice: hire a clerk, merge the inventory, find something unique to sell. Anything but what he was doing now.

The gnome's face lit with gratitude. He promised that the next time they visited, they wouldn't even recognize the place.

The party set out into the wilderness, heading southeast toward the mountains where Skree made his lair. The wind bit at their faces, and the snow fell in thick, swirling curtains.

Halfway to their destination, Aronious spotted tracks in the snow — fresh, large, unmistakable. Owlbear.

He followed the prints to a small grove of pines, where the beast hunched over the carcass of a dead elk, tearing into it with savage hunger. Aronious crept closer, but a twig snapped beneath his boot. The owlbear's head jerked up, eyes scanning the treeline. With a flick of his wrist, Aronious hurled a stone into the opposite brush. The creature turned, distracted, and he slipped away unseen.

They pressed onward.

By the time they reached the cave, the storm had grown fierce. The entrance yawned before them, dark and uninviting, the air inside thick with the stench of rot and decay.

They had barely stepped across the threshold when the chittering began.

Three massive spiders emerged from the shadows, their legs clicking against stone, their eyes gleaming with hunger. Yin Shen's breath caught — her arachnophobia flared hot and sharp — but she steadied herself with a few deep breaths. Then her hands ignited with flame, and she hurled bolts of fire into the dark with reckless abandon.

Hobbins' chromatic orb obliterated the carapace of the nearest spider in a burst of elemental fury. Aronious loosed arrows while his dire wolf tore into another, fangs flashing in the dim light. Yin Shen reached into her satchel and drew forth a small, furry ball. She hurled it at the farthest spider, and in a flash of magic, a giant badger materialized — snarling, clawing, biting with primal ferocity — before falling to the spider's venomous bite.

When the last spider fell, silence returned.

The party stood at the mouth of the cave, breath misting in the cold, the storm raging behind them and the dark unknown stretching ahead. Somewhere deeper within, Skree waited.

And they would find him.

Session 11: The Awakened Depths

After the chaos of the morning race, Yin Shen approached the bartender at The Wet Trout and asked about blacksmiths in town. He directed her to The Coal Poker, located on the other side of Easthaven near The White Lady Inn and the town stables.

Meanwhile, Hobbins bounced from table to table, asking the tavern-goers if they knew of any interesting stories or individuals looking for help from adventurers. Most were fishermen still up from the night before — red-eyed and half-asleep at their posts. But one fisherman leaned in conspiratorially and spoke of a personal encounter with a sea monster in Maer Dualdon while working as a fisher for the town of Bremen.

Exhausted from their race across town, the party returned to The White Lady Inn. Hobbins and Aronious collapsed into their beds and were asleep the moment their heads touched the pillows. But Yin Shen found herself restless. She rose and made her way to The Coal Poker.

Inside, she met the lead blacksmith — the largest dwarf she had ever seen, nearly human-sized but stronger and stockier. She explained that she was an investigator for the town, trying to learn all she could about Chardalyn. The blacksmith knew nothing of the material and showed clear distaste when Yin Shen mentioned the Duergar. He was not reluctant to help — merely direct and to-the-point in his speech. He muttered under his breath that it would have been nice to still have that wizard around, shame he'd turned out to be a criminal and been executed.

Yin Shen confirmed he was speaking of Dzaan — the red-robed wizard the party had briefly encountered and later witnessed burn at the stake. The blacksmith's expression darkened, and he revealed something unexpected: there had been a single survivor of Dzaan's expedition.

Randy Grailden, the survivor, was recovering at The White Lady Inn. The blacksmith suggested Randy might be able to show the party the way to Dzaan's tower.

Yin Shen returned to the inn and found Randy in the common area, scarfing down food with barely a breath between bites. She sat down beside him without ceremony and asked outright: "What can you tell me about Dzaan?"

Randy clammed up immediately, his hands beginning to shake — but he kept eating. "I don't know nothing," he stammered. "He took me, is all."

Yin Shen pressed him, back and forth, until she finally convinced him that she knew he'd been to the tower, knew he'd made it back alive and alone, and knew that everyone had a price.

Meekly, Randy agreed. "Fifteen gold to get out of this hell hole," he muttered to himself, "and five more to keep me comfy for a short while... yes, yes, that should do." He turned back to Yin Shen and asked for twenty gold.

She paid without hesitation. Randy pulled out her map and sketched the route to Dzaan's tower with a trembling hand.

Satisfied, Yin Shen finally retired to her room and slept.

In the morning, the party reconnected and decided their next heading should be Bremen to investigate the monster. They hired a caravan and made the long journey, swapping between sleeping and scanning the endless, stark white landscape.

When they arrived in Bremen, the party headed for the first door they saw. Hobbins barged in — and by sheer luck, the first building was the town's inn: Buried Treasures. A hearty dwarf greeted them warmly, but Hobbins cut him off, asking if he could point them toward the Speaker.

The dwarf smiled, thumped his chest, and gestured to a nearby table. "Oi, I am the Speaker 'ere. Wha' tis it you're needin'?"

Hobbins explained the rumor of a monster causing damage to fishing operations. The Speaker visibly relaxed, relieved to have someone take the issue seriously. He confirmed that yes, there was a monster in the lake — it was damaging boats and poaching fish.

As the conversation continued, a scholarly half-elf approached and interrupted. "This isn't a monster," she said firmly. "It's a Plesiosaurus. I've been trying to study it for some time now. I'd love to get my hands on any additional information." She introduced herself as Tali.

Together, the Speaker and Tali explained what they knew: the creature had a faint blue aura, appeared to be awakened, and radiated a pervasive feeling of unease whenever it was near.

As the conversation wound down, Hobbins asked for the Speaker's name. With a wide, toothy grin, he replied, "Dorbulgruf Shalescar. 'Bout time we got acquainted."

Hobbins then asked if there was a library in Bremen — she wanted to learn more about gods and divine energy. Dorbulgruf brought them to the town hall and opened the records room. It was sparse — just a single shelf of books — but it was enough. Hobbins leapt onto the shelves, scanning the spines until she found what she needed: Nature and the Divine.

She read voraciously, learning all about divine energy and godlings. They acted as conduits, and should the vessel for divine energy be destroyed, the energy would disperse or find a new host. An idea began to form in her mind: perhaps she could funnel divine energy into Lupine.

The party borrowed a rowboat from the docks and ventured out into the lake, fighting through ice floes to gather more information on the creature.

Hobbins and Yin Shen found themselves near the front of the boat, providing the main paddling and thrust, while Aronious used his ranger's intuition to guide them through the crushing ice. A sudden surge of water caused a chunk of ice to smash into the hull, splintering a portion of the boat. No water came in, and the group pressed onward.

They encountered a few fishing vessels here and there, steering clear of most. But one vessel from Targos shouted jeers at them, demanding they turn back. In defiance, the party continued and closed the gap.

The fishermen were angry, complaining that the party was stealing fish or scaring them off. Hobbins told them all to shut their trap and tossed a gold coin into their ship. A brawl immediately erupted among the crew, all scrambling for the coin. The captain wrestled it from a crewman's hand, pocketed it, and bellowed for them to correct course and find more fish elsewhere.

After more time adrift, a large wave suddenly blasted their boat, nearly tipping it over. Hobbins was quick to caution everyone that something wasn't right.

Then they saw it — a large shape gliding just below the surface, testing them.

The party decided to lure it toward the ice so they could stand on solid ground and ambush it. They rowed back to the ice floes and disembarked onto a large sheet of ice, hauling the boat up after them. They waited.

Finally, fins broke the surface.

Aronious decided to use speak with animals and called out that they had food.

A long, lizard-like head lifted from the water. In clear, Common speech, it said: "I'm listening."

A short conversation followed. Each additional voice seemed to agitate the beast further. The party learned that the creature had been awakened by a frost druid of Auril named Ravison. She had awakened multiple animals across Ten-Towns and commanded this one to make the people of Bremen suffer. So it had.

The creature believed that if it stopped, the spell would break and it would become a mindless beast again.

Hobbins, drawing on her extensive magical knowledge, was able to convince the creature that the awakening was permanent — that there was nothing binding it to this location or forcing it to obey.

With that, the creature slowly descended back into the water and shot off in the opposite direction, away from Bremen.

The party made their way back to town and found Speaker Dorbulgruf in his office, three empty tankards beside him and his cheeks and nose rosy with drink. He greeted them warmly and asked them to recount what happened.

They explained everything. Dorbulgruf handed Hobbins a journal. "Write down whatcha learnt," he said. "The elf lass needs her notes."

Hobbins scribbled furiously, her paws deft and quick. Everything they had learned was now inked onto the page.

The Speaker grabbed a small bundle from another drawer. "'Ere, for the trouble," he said, handing them a Scroll of Animal Friendship and a small pouch containing five gold pieces.

The party pocketed their rewards, exchanged farewells with the Speaker, and stepped back out into the frozen streets of Bremen — one mystery solved, and many more waiting in the cold.

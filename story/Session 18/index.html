Session 18: The Goblin's Crown

The new sled creaked and the six scrawny dogs strained against their leads as the party pushed out of Bremen and into the open tundra. The amulet in Aronious's hands pulsed with direction, a steady tug toward the mountains like a compass needle finding north.

The snow came down thick and fast. A blizzard rolled in without warning, reducing the world to a white wall pressing in on all sides. The party pressed on regardless, guided more by the amulet's pull than by any sense of where the land ended and the sky began.

After some time, a glow materialized in the white distance — faint at first, then unmistakable. Curiosity won out, and the sled veered toward it.

The light resolved into a torch held aloft by a stout dwarven figure trudging through waist-deep drifts with the single-minded energy of a man on a mission he's thoroughly enjoying. He was barrel-chested, trailing a long red beard packed with snow, and strapped across his back was a peculiar device — a metal tube mounted in a wooden frame, a hemp wick hanging from the end. None of them recognized the contraption.

He greeted them with the enthusiasm of someone who had been alone in a blizzard too long and found it wholly acceptable.

His name was Mylbor Tarrfak — a trophy hunter with a cabin to the northeast, currently in pursuit of a Red Yeti that had been making a nuisance of itself in the area. The device on his back, he explained with some pride, was his "Thunder Stick" — a single shot, but one that packed a punch. He offered the party a wager: three hundred gold if they could find the Yeti before he did.

They declined.

Yin Shen asked if he had any interest in hunting a dragon instead. Mylbor's eyes lit — then dimmed slightly when she described the Chardalyn Dragon as a construct. He was on the verge of losing interest entirely when she mentioned Duergar.

The color rose in his face like a tide.

"That certainly sweetens the deal."

They agreed to reconnect in two days at the tavern in Bremen, and his torch faded to a dim amber point behind the curtain of snow.

The amulet led the party onward toward the mountains. The terrain shifted, the wind dropping as they descended into a ravine carved into the rock — sheltered from the storm above, growing darker as the walls rose around them. The sky narrowed to a pale strip overhead.

The ravine opened without warning into an interior clearing. Goblin construction sprawled across it — watchtowers, timber walls, the whole crude architecture of a settlement dug in and making itself permanent. The guards at their posts were paying far more attention to the braziers beside them than to the perimeter they were supposed to be watching.

Aronious could feel the Shield Guardian somewhere at the center.

Yin Shen decided the most efficient path was the direct one. She walked toward the gates as though she had every right to be there — until an arrow snapped into the ground in front of her feet and a goblin voice demanded to know her business, with the clear implication that the next shot would not be aimed at the dirt.

She announced they were expected.

The effect was immediate. The goblin ran. Yellow eyes narrowed at them from the walls as bows were drawn — and then the great gates groaned open to reveal a larger goblin astride a worg, studying them with the measured expression of someone weighing a decision.

"Are you the emissaries?"

Yin Shen agreed that they were.

Behind her, Polard Hinscrop was vibrating. Hobbins asked what was wrong. He explained, in a frantic whisper, that goblins and gnomes were natural enemies — that the moment they got a proper look at him, things would go sideways in a hurry. Hobbins climbed to his shoulders and wrapped him in a disguise: not ideal, but a dwarf was better odds than a gnome.

They were escorted inside.

At the center of the stronghold, the Shield Guardian hung from rope and timber like a puppet with its strings cut — massive, still, and waiting. Hobbins broke from the group and slipped unseen toward it while the rest continued to the chief's building, Yin Shen taking up the thread of the disguise as Hobbins moved away.

The chief's hall was dim, and the goblin who occupied it was a peculiar specimen — unsettling in ways that were hard to place. He led them into a room he called the "chatting chamber" and closed the door behind them.

Then he reached up and pulled off his head.

Beneath the goblin mask was a gnome — lean, harried, and profoundly relieved to see someone who wasn't a goblin. He reintroduced himself as Spellix Romwood, a merchant whose caravan had been ambushed in the mountain pass to the west. His goods were gone, his people taken, and he had fashioned a goblin disguise to try to free them. Somehow — through a series of events he described as "immediately going wrong in every direction" — he had stumbled into the role of chief. He was now certain that several of the goblin elders had seen through the ruse and were quietly plotting his removal.

He asked for help getting out alive. The party agreed, since they wanted what hung in the center of the camp.

A plan was made. They would stay until dusk, wait for the goblins to bed down and the guards to grow careless, then move on the Shield Guardian in the dark.

It went poorly from the moment the construct activated.

An alarm rang through the stronghold. Goblins boiled from their quarters, grabbing weapons; worgs were cut loose from their pens; the shamans were roused. Whatever order the night had held dissolved in an instant.

Hobbins moved through it like a force of nature. Chromatic orbs blazed from her paws in rapid succession, each one finding its mark with devastating finality. Aronious summoned his spirit guardian and from the ether, an Icewind Dale reindeer manifested beside him — a spirit of the wild, pure and fierce. He loosed arrows across the battlefield with a ranger's economy of motion, his thorn whip dragging goblins to the ground in bursts of writhing vine.

Yin Shen cut through the goblin buildings, locating Polard, Dzaan, and Spellix and driving them toward the wall. She reached the battlements, secured a rope and grappling hook, and planted herself at the exit corridor to hold it.

Hobbins looked back at the stronghold.

Then she set it on fire.

Dzaan, still hollowed out with exhaustion, crept along the battle's outer edge toward Yin Shen.

Polard walked out of the building and squared up to the volleys of arrows raining down on his companions. Something shifted in him then.

"Not at my friends!"

His armor shimmered. Every seam sealed and melted flush, the pasta strainer and bent cymbals and kitchen implements fusing into one immaculate whole — and then the whole grew, the armor swelling to ten feet tall as a deep resonant voice rolled outward from within it. A lightning hammer ignited in his fist, and Polard Hinscrop threw himself into the fray, the hammer rising and falling like a conductor calling for more.

Aronious felt the connection to the Shield Guardian thrum through the amulet in his grip. He reached for it and commanded the construct to defend them. The ten-foot golem lurched into motion and began methodically demolishing anything in range.

Then the stronghold truly woke.

Every door, every shutter, every dark gap in the timber walls disgorged goblins at once. Not a patrol. Not a garrison. A horde — screaming and surging across the clearing in a tide that swallowed the firelight. Arrows came not in volleys but in walls, a constant hissing sheet of them filling the air above the courtyard.

The worgs hit hardest. They came low and fast, coordinated in a way that felt almost deliberate — circling, splitting attention, striking from directions the party couldn't cover all at once. Two of them drove into Aronious in succession, jaws finding purchase through leather and fur. He threw one off, and a grazing arrow opened a line of fire across his shoulder. Another shaft caught him in the thigh. He staggered, still loosing, still standing — but the ground beneath him was beginning to feel uncertain.

Polard fared no better at the center. The worgs read him as the greatest threat and converged accordingly, four of the beasts surrounding him in snarling, snapping chaos. His hammer swung and connected — one worg crumpled and did not rise — but the others pressed in, teeth finding the gaps where his patchwork armor joined.

It was the Shield Guardian that broke it open.

The construct read the battlefield with the cold clarity of something that did not feel fear or exhaustion. It stepped between Aronious and the next volley without hesitation, its enormous frame absorbing a dozen arrows as though they were rain. It turned its back to the watchmen on the walls and spread itself wide, a ten-foot bulwark of ancient stone and iron, Hobbins sheltered in its shadow as arrows clattered harmlessly off the construct's shoulders. When the worgs surged again toward Polard, the Guardian's gauntleted hand swept two of them from the ground entirely.

But the arrows did not stop. More goblins filled the ramparts for every one that fell, and Aronious was moving wrong — slower, each step deliberate in a way that meant something was badly wrong beneath his furs. Hobbins clutched a wound at her side where a shaft had grazed through, her breathing tight.

This was not a battle they could win.

Yin Shen made the call before anyone else could. She shouted the order to move and put herself at the foot of the rope, blade up, holding the exit with everything she had as the others came to her.

Dzaan reached her first. Then Spellix. Then Polard, his great form shrinking back to its natural size as he ran, the hammer winking out. Hobbins came next, fast and low through the smoke and firelight, and then Aronious — moving on grit alone now, the Guardian a looming wall of shadow behind him, still turning arrows, still holding the line long enough for its master to reach the rope.

They descended into the dark outside the walls one by one. As Yin Shen dropped and hit the ground, the shouts from within the stronghold split — half still howling pursuit, half now screaming at the fire spreading hungrily through the timber buildings behind them.

The party ran. The dogsled waited in the ravine. The Guardian fell in behind them, its great footfalls steady in the snow.

By the time the ravine opened onto open tundra and the cold air hit their lungs, the pursuit had died to distant shouting. They did not slow. Bremen lay ahead in the dark, and the road between them and it was long and cold and mercifully empty.

Behind them, somewhere in the mountain, the stronghold burned.

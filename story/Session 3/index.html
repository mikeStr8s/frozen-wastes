Session 3: The Prophecy at Caer-Dineval

The morning after the séance brought no answers, only the lingering echo of a spirit's whisper: Caer-Dineval. By dawn, the decision was made.

Yin Shen spent the early hours searching for any trace of the White Lady's presence, but found nothing. The spirit had returned to whatever cold deep claimed it. By midday, the trio had secured passage on a caravan heading north along the frozen lakeshore.

When Caer-Dineval came into view, it was not welcoming. The town clung to the cliffs, and above it loomed the Caer itself — a fortress of dark stone, austere and forbidding, its towers crowned with wind-shredded banners.

The party made their way to The Uphill Climb, a tavern perched northwest of the castle. Inside, the proprietor Roark served hot chowder but had no beer or spirits to offer. Yin Shen asked about the White Lady. Roark knew the ghost story but little else. When asked who ran the town, Roark told them the Speaker was Crannoc Siever, a human who kept to the Caer these days.

The Caer's entrance was barred by thick wooden doors, its guard towers rising like sentinels above. Aronious called up, requesting an audience with the Speaker.

A voice rang out from above, sharp and cold, telling them the Speaker was unwell and saw no one. When Yin Shen pressed, three arrows hissed through the air in warning. One grazed Aronious's shoulder. More guards appeared at the arrow slits, bows drawn.

Hobbins' whiskers twitched with mischief. Before Yin Shen could stop her, the ferret cast her spell. High above, a guard named Henry Davis blinked, suddenly certain these travelers were trustworthy friends. He convinced his captain to let them in, and the great doors groaned open.

Henry led them through cold stone corridors to the Speaker's office. The room was empty save for tax documents and other parchment scattered on the desk. Hobbins found a book — The History of Caer-Dineval — while Yin Shen asked Henry where the Speaker was. The low-ranking guard didn't know, but suggested they speak with the soothsayer, Hethyl Arkorran.

Hethyl sat hunched by a brazier, her skin like parchment, one eye missing, and a peg where her right leg would have been. When asked who Hethyl and the guards were, she told them they were the Knights of the Black Sword. A name unfamiliar to the party.

Hethyl had little time for idle talk and stressed to the party that Duergar are the danger. They must be stopped. Hethyl's eyes grew distant and clouded. Her voice became something older, colder, threaded with prophecy:

"Hidden away, buried in the mountains, lies a fortress, a villain, a crystal machine with a dragon's heart! O, mighty wings beating and breath like the sun! Bright enough to BURN away the Rime — to burn us all if you do... not... stop..."

With a final, rattling breath, Hethyl Arkorran went still. Dead.

The party sent Henry to fetch the captain. When he returned with a dozen armed guards, they all tried to explain at once — their words tumbling over each other in a garbled mess. The captain's gaze moved from the dead soothsayer to the travelers, then to Henry, still smiling and standing too close to the strangers.

The captain's jaw tightened.

"Restrain them," he said coldly, then pointed at Henry. "And clap that traitor in irons."

The guards moved forward. The moment hung frozen — a breath before the fall.
